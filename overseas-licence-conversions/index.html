Who these lessons help

Driving support for different overseas licence pathways

Service NSW decides each applicant's licence pathway, while Driving School Liverpool Centre provides the practical coaching that helps eligible drivers feel prepared on NSW roads. A driver who finds left-side traffic genuinely frightening can start with driving lessons for nervous drivers.

Experienced overseas drivers

A driver with years of overseas experience can book a focused assessment lesson, so familiar vehicle control is retained and lesson time targets NSW-specific habits.

Drivers with a test requirement

A licence holder who has been told to complete a NSW driving test can practise test-standard observation, speed choice, lane position and manoeuvres before the booked assessment.

Temporary overseas visitors

A temporary visa holder applying for a NSW licence can build local-road confidence while Service NSW confirms the documents, deadlines and tests that apply to their circumstances.

Permanent residents and citizens

A permanent resident or Australian citizen transferring an overseas licence can use lessons to identify local driving differences before any knowledge or practical test Service NSW requires.

Drivers used to right-side traffic

A driver moving from right-side traffic can rehearse left-side positioning, turns, roundabouts and lane selection gradually on quieter roads before entering complex traffic.

International drivers rebuilding confidence

A driver who feels uncertain in a new road system can begin with a calm refresher lesson and add busier intersections, parking or motorway driving at a comfortable pace.

Current NSW requirements

Check what Service NSW requires before you book

Overseas licence rules depend on personal circumstances and can change, so official guidance should decide your deadline, documents and tests before lesson planning begins.

Conversion timeframes are different

Service NSW currently says Australian citizens, permanent residents and New Zealand licence holders generally have three months to transfer an eligible overseas licence if they want to keep driving in NSW.

Temporary overseas visitors who arrived in NSW on or after 1 July 2023 and intend to live in NSW for more than six months generally need a temporary NSW licence within six months if they want to keep driving. Service NSW should confirm how the rule applies to you.

Tests depend on the licence and applicant

Service NSW may require a Driver Knowledge Test, Hazard Perception Test, practical Driving Test or other checks depending on the issuing country or jurisdiction, the applicant's age, licence history and current eligibility.

Driving School Liverpool Centre can prepare you for practical driving, but it cannot determine exemptions, validate overseas documents, translate a licence, book government tests on your behalf or guarantee a conversion outcome.

  • Bring your current licence or other legal authority to drive to every lesson.
  • Tell the instructor about any licence condition and wear any required glasses or contact lenses.
  • Confirm government documents, translations, fees and test bookings directly with Service NSW.

An International Driving Permit is not a NSW licence

NSW Government guidance says a driver whose overseas licence is not in English must also carry an English translation or an International Driving Permit while eligible to drive as a visitor. Service NSW decides which translations and documents it accepts for a NSW licence application.
