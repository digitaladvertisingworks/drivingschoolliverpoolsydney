A repeatable parking method

See it, practise it, then use it somewhere new

Ashraf or George breaks the manoeuvre into visible decisions, because a driver needs a method they can adjust when the space, kerb or neighbouring vehicles change.

1

Choose a safe starting place

Your instructor begins on a wide, low-traffic street or quiet car park in your service area, where you can work at walking pace without pressure from waiting traffic.

2

Set the car and observation routine

You learn the starting position, mirror view, signal and blind-spot checks that must happen before the car changes direction.

3

Steer through clear reference points

You reverse slowly, pause when needed and use simple visual markers to understand when the car should turn, straighten and stop.

4

Correct the position safely

Your instructor shows you how to recognise a poor angle early, move forward or back under control and finish without rushing a second attempt.

5

Repeat in a different space

You apply the same decisions beside a different kerb or in a different bay, which turns one successful attempt into a transferable parking skill.

What makes parking reliable

Build the decisions around the steering

Parking is more than turning the wheel, so every lesson combines low-speed vehicle control with observation, legal position and a safe way to leave the space.

Mirrors and blind spots

Check around the car before and during movement so pedestrians, cyclists and approaching vehicles are noticed while there is still time to stop.

Walking-pace control

Balance the brake and steering in the automatic car so the vehicle moves slowly enough for accurate positioning and immediate stopping.

Kerb and bay judgement

Use the mirrors and surrounding lines to judge distance from the kerb, neighbouring cars and the front or rear boundary of the space.

Signals and communication

Signal before pulling over, parking or leaving the kerb so other road users can understand the movement before the car changes position.

Safe corrections

Pause, observe and reposition when the approach is wrong, because an early correction is safer and more useful than forcing the car into a poor angle.

Leaving the space

Recheck mirrors and blind spots, indicate and move only when the path is clear, so the parking routine includes the exit rather than ending when the car stops.
