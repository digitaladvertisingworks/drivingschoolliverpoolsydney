Straight answers first

What a beginner automatic driving lesson involves

A beginner driving lesson at Driving School Liverpool Centre is a one-to-one automatic lesson taught by a Transport for NSW accredited instructor, starting on a quiet residential street within a few minutes of the learner's own home.

The facts a first-time learner asks for

A learner who has never driven begins with the engine off, covering seat position, mirrors, steering grip and the pedals, then moves off and stops on a low-traffic street chosen for width and visibility. Ashraf teaches this first hour across the Liverpool region and George teaches it across Campbelltown and Macarthur, and each learner keeps the same instructor and the same automatic car from that first hour to test day.

  • Cost: $80 for one hour, $150 for two hours, with free door-to-door pick-up and drop-off included.
  • Length: one hour is enough for a genuine first drive; two hours suits a learner who wants to repeat a new skill in the same session.
  • Requirement: a current NSW learner licence, which you get by passing the Driver Knowledge Test at Service NSW.
  • Car: a dual-control automatic, so the instructor can intervene while you are still building control.
  • Logbook: eligible learners under 25 record three logbook hours for each structured instructor hour, up to 10 professional hours.
  • Where: quiet streets in your own suburb across Liverpool, Campbelltown, Macarthur and the Camden estates.
  • When: seven days a week, 7am to 7pm, including weekends and school holidays.

Your first hours behind the wheel

Everything a first-time driver actually starts with

Driving School Liverpool Centre teaches beginners in an automatic car with a Transport for NSW accredited instructor, so a learner who has never driven begins on a quiet local street rather than in traffic. A beginner who dreads that first hour can book driving lessons for nervous drivers instead, which start slower again.

Set the car up for you

Your instructor shows you seat position, mirrors, steering grip and the pedals in a stationary car in your own street, because a driver who is set up correctly can see and reach everything before the car moves.

Move off and stop smoothly

You practise moving off, gentle braking and stopping at a chosen point on a low-traffic road in Chipping Norton or Kearns, which is the skill that turns a nervous first drive into a comfortable one.

Steer, look and place the car

Ashraf and George build steering control, mirror checks and lane position on wide residential streets, so you learn to keep the car where you want it while you are looking well ahead.

Why beginners choose automatic

One less thing to think about on your first drive

An automatic car removes the clutch and gear changes, which frees a new learner to spend their attention on the road, the mirrors and the decisions that keep them safe.

Attention goes to the road

A beginner in an automatic watches traffic, signs and pedestrians instead of managing a clutch, so the observation habits that matter on test day form from the very first lesson.

The same car every lesson

You learn in one familiar automatic car with the same instructor each time, which means the controls, mirrors and seating position never change while you are still building confidence.

Check the licence condition

A driving test passed in an automatic car carries a licence condition, so confirm the current rule with Service NSW before you choose, and your instructor will talk it through with you first.

Where beginners start

Quiet local streets, chosen on purpose

A first lesson works best on a wide, calm road with clear kerb lines and light traffic, so your instructor picks the starting street around where you live rather than using one route for everyone.

In the Liverpool region, Ashraf starts beginners on streets such as Wilby Street and Derby Crescent in Chipping Norton, then adds the roundabouts and connectors around Casula and Prestons once steering and stopping feel reliable.

Across Macarthur, George uses the quiet crescents of Kearns, Raby and Woodbine for first drives, then builds toward Ingleburn and the Campbelltown town centre as your observation and speed choice improve.

Later lessons progress toward the Service NSW test area you will eventually use, whether that is Warwick Farm, Edmondson Park, Macquarie Fields or Macarthur, so nothing about the roads is new when you book a test.

A calm street is a teaching tool

A beginner who can steer and stop confidently on a quiet road is ready for traffic much sooner.

Where beginner lessons lead

From your first drive to your driving test

Beginner lessons are the first stage of a plan that continues all the way to a Service NSW test booking, and each stage below builds directly on the one before it.

Logbook hours that count for more

Learners under 25 can record eligible instructor hours at the NSW three-for-one rate, so early professional lessons build your logbook faster than supervised practice alone.

How many hours a NSW learner needs

The learner hours guide sets out the 120-hour total, the 20 night hours and the age-25 exemption in one page you can read with a parent before booking.

Driving test preparation and practice tests

When your control is steady, your instructor moves you onto test-standard observation, manoeuvres and a full practice run of your local Service NSW route.

Parking practice lessons

Build reverse parallel, 90-degree and angle parking through clear reference points once low-speed steering and observation feel steady.

Wet weather driving

Rain is part of ordinary driving, so lessons add real wet roads deliberately once you are comfortable on dry daytime surfaces.

Night driving lessons

Evening lessons after sunset cover headlights, glare and judging distance in low light, and they start the 20 night hours a learner under 25 must log.

Lesson prices and packages

Single hours suit a first drive, and multi-hour packages suit a learner building steady weekly progress toward a test booking.

Weekend driving lessons

A first-time learner still at school or working full time can take the same beginner lesson on a quiet Saturday or Sunday morning, when local streets are calmer and there is room for two hours.

Driving test car hire for test day

Once the test is booked, you can hire the same automatic car you learned in and take a warm-up lesson on the roads around your Service NSW centre beforehand.
