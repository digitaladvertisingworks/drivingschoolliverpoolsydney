What the NSW log book asks for

The driver hours a NSW learner has to record

Service NSW asks a learner driver under 25 to record supervised driving in the Learner Driver Log Book before booking the practical driving test, and the six cards below set out exactly what those hours have to show.

120 supervised hours under 25

A learner driver under 25 records at least 120 hours of supervised driving in the log book before the practical driving test, and every hour driven with Ashraf in the Liverpool region or George Hany in Campbelltown and Macarthur is written up before you get out of the car.

20 of those hours after dark

At least 20 of the 120 hours are driven between sunset and sunrise, which is why Driving School Liverpool Centre runs evening bookings until 7pm across Liverpool, Casula, Campbelltown and Oran Park all year round.

Twelve months on your learner licence

A learner under 25 holds the learner licence for at least 12 months before moving to a P1 licence, so the smoothest plan spreads roughly 10 hours of driving across each of those months rather than saving them for the end.

Learners aged 25 and over

Service NSW does not ask a learner aged 25 or over to submit log book hours, and those drivers usually book a short block of lessons with Ashraf or George to reach a confident, test-ready standard instead.

Where the hours are recorded

Hours are kept in the digital log book inside the Service NSW app or in the paper Learner Driver Log Book, and your instructor fills in the date, start and finish times, kilometres, conditions and their accreditation number for every lesson.

Who can supervise home practice

A supervising driver holds a full Australian driver licence for that class of vehicle, so a parent, older sibling or family friend on a provisional licence cannot sign your log book, while an accredited instructor always can.

3-for-1 logbook hours

How one lesson hour becomes three logbook hours

A learner driver under 25 who takes a structured lesson with a Transport for NSW accredited instructor can have each eligible hour recorded as three hours in the log book, up to the published limit, which is the fastest legitimate way to build the 120 total while the driving itself improves. The full NSW learner hours guide explains the rule in detail.

The precise limit

  • A maximum of 10 actual instructor hours can attract the bonus credit.
  • Those 10 hours can be recorded as 30: 10 actual hours plus 20 bonus hours.
  • You may take more than 10 professional lessons, but no further 3-for-1 credit applies to them.
  • Bonus hours from an evening lesson are recorded as daytime hours, so only the time you actually drive after sunset counts toward the 20 night hours.
  • Service NSW sets the current rules on hours, exemptions and credits rather than the driving school.

Your 20 night hours

The night hours are the ones learners leave too late

Night hours are the hours driven between sunset and sunrise, and they are the part of the log book a family cannot catch up on quickly, because a summer evening in Liverpool stays light until well after dinner.

Ashraf and George Hany book evening lessons against the actual sunset time for the season, so a 5.30pm start in June fills your night column while the same booking in December does not. Winter is the cheapest time to bank night hours, and both instructors teach until 7pm seven days a week.

A night lesson does more than move a number. Learners practise low beam and high beam choice on unlit stretches of Newbridge Road, recovering from oncoming glare on Appin Road, judging following distance under street lights on Governor Macquarie Drive, and spotting pedestrians on the Campbelltown and Casula shopping streets where dark clothing appears late.

The 3-for-1 bonus hours from an eligible evening lesson are recorded as daytime hours, so only the time you actually drive after sunset counts toward the 20-hour night requirement. Your instructor writes both figures into the log book at the end of the lesson so the two columns stay correct.

Night hours you cannot borrow

A learner who starts evening lessons in the first few months finishes the 20 night hours early instead of chasing dark roads the month a test is booked.

Keeping the log book right

Small habits that keep your log book accurate

Service NSW asks a learner and their supervising driver to declare log book hours accurately, and an honest, complete book is also the one that shows a testing officer a genuinely prepared driver.

Record each drive as it finishes, while the start time, finish time and distance are still in front of you, and keep the digital log book in the Service NSW app updated rather than transferring a month of drives from memory.

Vary what you record as well as how much. A book full of one easy route around a single suburb builds a smaller driver than the same hours spread across quiet streets, arterial roads, rain, night driving and unfamiliar suburbs.

Record every drive with

  • The date, the start time and the finish time of the drive
  • The kilometres driven and the road and light conditions
  • The supervising driver's name and licence number, or your instructor's accreditation number
  • Night hours entered in the night column, not the daytime total
  • Your own driving goal for the next drive, so the following hour has a purpose

Who decides what

Service NSW owns the log book rules, we own the teaching

Reading this before you book keeps the two roles clear, so nothing about your log book comes as a surprise in the month you want to sit the driving test.

Service NSW sets the hours, the exemptions and the credits

Driving School Liverpool Centre provides accredited instruction, completes your log book for the lessons you take and prepares you for the practical driving test. Service NSW sets the current requirements for supervised hours, night hours, exemptions, the 3-for-1 credit and how the log book is submitted, and confirms your eligibility before a test appointment is issued.

  • Check your own age, exemption and licence conditions with Service NSW rather than assuming a rule you heard from another family.
  • The 3-for-1 credit applies to eligible structured lessons with a Transport for NSW accredited instructor, up to a maximum of 10 actual hours.
  • No lesson, package or completed log book decides a driving test result; the testing officer assesses your driving on the day.

Accurate hours protect the learner who drove them

A log book is a declaration, and Service NSW expects the learner and the supervising driver to record only the driving that actually happened. Driving School Liverpool Centre records the exact time you drive with your instructor, including the eligible bonus hours, and will not sign for hours you did not drive.

  • Keep the digital log book in the Service NSW app or the paper book up to date after each drive.
  • Ask your instructor if an entry looks wrong, because a correction made the same week is simple to explain.

Every lesson is in an automatic car

Driving School Liverpool Centre teaches learner drivers in automatic vehicles across Liverpool, Campbelltown and Macarthur. Hours driven in an automatic count in your log book in the ordinary way, and passing the practical test in an automatic gives you a NSW licence carrying an automatic condition, which Service NSW explains in full. A learner who needs a manual licence sits the test in a manual vehicle arranged elsewhere.
