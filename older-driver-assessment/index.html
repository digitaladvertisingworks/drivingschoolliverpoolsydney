What the assessment is

The NSW older driver assessment, explained plainly

Transport for NSW asks drivers aged 85 and over to pass a practical driving assessment every two years to keep an unrestricted licence. Driving School Liverpool Centre does not conduct that assessment. We teach the driving it looks at, so you arrive on the day having already done the drive.

A drive on ordinary roads

An authorised older driver assessor sits beside you for a drive on normal suburban and arterial roads, watching how you observe, position the car and choose your speed, rather than setting you tricks or asking for anything unusual.

Booked through Service NSW

You book the practical driving assessment through Service NSW with an authorised older driver assessor, and Service NSW alone decides the result. A driving school lesson prepares you for that drive and never replaces it.

Practice you can do first

Nothing stops you rehearsing the same roads, turns and manoeuvres beforehand with a Transport for NSW accredited instructor, which is exactly what a Driving School Liverpool Centre older driver assessment lesson is for.

What NSW asks, and when

Licence milestones for older drivers in NSW

The requirements below come from the Transport for NSW older driver licensing system and change with your age, not with your postcode. Confirm your own dates with Service NSW, because the letter about your licence is the document that counts.

What the assessor watches

The driving an older driver assessment actually looks at

An older driver assessment is not a test of how long you have held your licence. The assessor watches the same safe-driving behaviours all day, and every one of them can be practised in advance with your instructor.

Head checks and mirror routine

The item older drivers most often lose marks on is the head check over the shoulder before a lane change or a merge, because decades of driving quietly replace it with a mirror glance. Your instructor rebuilds the full routine.

Seating position and comfortable movement

If turning to look over your shoulder has become uncomfortable, your instructor works on seat height, mirror setup and how far you actually need to turn, and will say honestly when the question belongs with your doctor or an occupational therapist.

Gap selection and giving way

Judging gaps at a roundabout on The Northern Road, or turning right across Newbridge Road at Moorebank, takes practice, and repeating the same intersection several times in one lesson is what makes the decision quick again.

Speed choice in changing zones

School zones, the 40km/h shopping strips in Liverpool and Campbelltown and the drop to 40km/h when passing a stationary emergency vehicle all get rehearsed, because an assessor marks the speed you did not notice as well as the speed you chose.

Reverse parking and manoeuvres

Reverse parallel parking, ninety-degree bays and three-point turns are practised against fixed reference points on quiet streets first, then repeated in a real shopping-centre car park where other cars are moving.

Driving calmly with someone beside you

Driving while a stranger writes on a clipboard is its own skill. Sitting beside a professional instructor for a few hours makes the assessor's presence ordinary instead of unnerving.

Assessment day

Arrive with everything sorted except the driving

Service NSW sends you the formal instructions for your practical driving assessment. This is the short, practical list drivers tell us they wish they had checked the week before.

Your assessment is driven in a car that is registered, roadworthy and insured, and most older drivers use their own vehicle because it is the one they know. Check the tyres, lights, indicators and windscreen a few days ahead, not on the morning.

Bring your licence, and your glasses if your licence names them as a condition. Book the drive for the time of day you normally drive, so the glare, traffic and energy levels are the ones you are used to, and keep a quiet half hour beforehand instead of arriving straight from something else.

Before assessment day

  • Current NSW licence, plus glasses or contact lenses if your licence requires them
  • The Service NSW letter showing your date, time and location
  • A registered, roadworthy car with working lights and indicators
  • A booking time that matches when you normally drive
  • One or two practice lessons on the roads near the centre
  • Any medical paperwork your doctor has asked you to carry

Before you book

What the driving school does, and what Service NSW does

Older driver assessment lessons are ordinary driving lessons taught at the school's published rates. A few points are worth being clear about before the first booking.

We prepare you, we do not assess you

Driving School Liverpool Centre teaches older driver assessment preparation as one-to-one lessons with a Transport for NSW accredited instructor. The school does not conduct the official older driver practical driving assessment, does not issue or renew licences and cannot change a licence condition.

  • Book the practical driving assessment itself through Service NSW with an authorised older driver assessor.
  • Medical assessments, medical reviews and licence conditions are handled by your doctor and Service NSW.
  • No lesson guarantees an assessment result, a licence renewal or an insurance outcome.

You need a current, valid licence

You do the driving in these lessons, so you must hold a current NSW or otherwise valid driver licence, and you must wear any glasses or contact lenses your licence names as a condition. Tell your instructor about any medical condition or medication that affects your driving before the first lesson.

  • Bring your licence to every lesson.
  • If your licence has expired or been suspended, contact Service NSW before booking a lesson.

Lesson prices, packages and cancellations

An older driver assessment lesson costs the same as any other lesson, so a single hour, a two-hour session or a multi-hour package is charged at the published rate. Most older drivers book between one and four hours in total.
